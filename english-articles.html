Overview

Articles in English

Articles help the listener work out whether a noun is specific or general.

There are three articles a an the, plus the zero article Ø when we use no article at all.

Ask a simple question every time you see a noun: am I talking about this thing in general, or about a specific one?

This guide breaks the system into chapters so the rules are easier to revise and compare.

Chapter 1

Indefinite Articles: a / an

Form

Use a before a consonant sound and an before a vowel sound. The sound matters, not the spelling.

a dog, a university, an apple, an hour

Main uses

  1. First mention of a singular countable noun: I saw a girl in the park.
  2. One, but not which one: I need a pen.
  3. Jobs and roles: She is a doctor.
  4. Classifying something: Paris is a beautiful city.
  5. Exclamations after what: What a day!

When not to use a / an

  • Plural nouns in general: Books are expensive.
  • Uncountable nouns in general: Water is essential.
  • Proper names: London, Italy, John.

You can use a / an with an uncountable noun when you mean a type or a serving: a coffee, a wine, a knowledge of...

Chapter 2

Definite Article: the

Core meaning

The points to something specific or known. The listener can identify the noun from context, previous mention, world knowledge, or a phrase that makes it specific.

  • Second mention: I bought a pen. The pen is black.
  • Shared situation: Close the window.
  • Unique things: the sun, the moon, the Earth.

Common patterns

  1. Noun already mentioned or defined.
  2. A phrase makes the noun specific: the mother of the child.
  3. Superlatives and ordinals: the best student, the first chapter.
  4. A whole specific group: the rich, the elderly.
  5. Specific uncountables: pass the salt.
  6. Many geographic names: the Nile, the Alps, the United States.

When not to use the

Do not use the for general plurals, general uncountables, or most proper names.

Dogs are friendly animals. Music helps me relax. John lives in France.

Chapter 3

Zero Article: Ø

The zero article means no article before the noun. That absence itself carries meaning.

General plurals and uncountables

  • Plural, general: Computers revolutionized communication.
  • Uncountable, general: Water is essential.

Proper names

Most proper nouns do not take an article: John, London, France, Japan, Asia, Lake Michigan, Mount Everest.

But some names take the: the United States, the Netherlands, the Himalayas.

Other common zero-article patterns

  • Languages and academic subjects: Spanish, history.
  • Meals: lunch, dinner.
  • Institutions used for their main purpose: school, hospital.
  • Transport and routine expressions: by car, on foot, at home, to work.

Chapter 5

Practical Revision Strategy

When you revise articles, train yourself to ask three questions for every noun.

  1. Is it countable or uncountable?
  2. Am I talking about it in general or a specific one?
  3. Is it singular, and do I mean one of many or this particular one?

If it is plural or uncountable in a general sense, the answer is often zero article. If it is specific, the answer is often the.

That three-question check is enough for most article decisions in ordinary English.
