Introduction

How to Use This Guide

Part 4 is not a guessing task.

Do not just put words in the gap.

First understand the meaning.
Then use the key word.
Then fit the answer into the whole sentence.
Then check it.

The routine in this guide is:

Meaning → Key Word → Gap Frame → Pattern → Answer → Check

You will see this routine many times. That is deliberate. In the exam, you need a habit, not a lucky guess.

The official B2 First Reading and Use of English Part 4 task has six questions plus an example. Each question gives you a first sentence, a key word, and a second sentence with a gap. You must complete the second sentence so that it has a similar meaning to the first sentence. Your answer must use the key word unchanged and must usually be two to five words, including the key word. Cambridge describes the focus as grammar and vocabulary, with a range of structures tested. Cambridge also explains that the skill is expressing a message in a different way.1

Cambridge teaching tips also say that you should write only the missing words, not the whole sentence, and that you should pay attention to time clues, singular/plural clues, and the words before and after the gap.2

Part 1 — Understand the Task
Chapter 1

What Part 4 Is

In Part 4, you see a sentence. Then you see a word in capital letters. Then you see another sentence with a gap. Your job is to complete the second sentence so that it means almost the same as the first sentence.

What You See

Example:

I did not phone Sarah because I was busy.

TOO

I was                  Sarah.

Answer
too busy to phone
I was too busy to phone Sarah.

Why This Works

The first sentence means:

I was busy. Because of this, I did not phone Sarah.

The second sentence says the same idea in another way:

I was too busy to phone Sarah.

The answer works because:

Check Yourself
  • Same main meaning? Yes.
  • Key word unchanged? Yes: too.
  • Two to five words? Yes: too busy to phone = four words.
  • Full sentence grammatical? Yes: I was too busy to phone Sarah.

Important Words for This Guide

Part 4 is a paraphrasing task. You are not writing a new idea. You are saying the same main idea in a different way.
Part 1 — Understand the Task
Chapter 2

The Four Rules

There are four main rules.

  1. Keep the same main meaning.
  2. Use the key word exactly.
  3. Write two to five words, including the key word.
  4. Make the whole second sentence grammatical.

One more practical rule:

Practical Rule
Write only the missing words, not the whole sentence.

Cambridge materials state that the answer must contain two to five words, must include the key word unchanged, and should be written as the words needed to fill the gap, not as the full second sentence.1

Rule 1: Keep the Same Main Meaning

You are not allowed to park here.

MUST

You                  here.

✗ Wrong Answer

do not have to park

Full sentence: You do not have to park here.

Problem: This means parking is not necessary. But the first sentence means parking is prohibited.

Correct Answer
must not park
You must not park here.
Common Mistake

don't have to = not necessary

must not = not allowed

Rule 2: Use the Key Word Exactly

She succeeded in finding a job.

SUCCESS

She                  a job.

✗ Wrong Answer

was successful in finding

Problem: The key word changed from success to successful.

Correct Style
had success in finding
She had success in finding a job.
Rule
Even if your grammar is good, changing the key word breaks the task rule.

Rule 3: Write Two to Five Words

I last met Ana in 2020.

SINCE

I                  2020.

Correct Answer
have not met Ana since
I have not met Ana since 2020.

Word count: have / not / met / Ana / since = five words

✗ Wrong Answer

have not had a meeting with Ana since

Problem: Too many words.

Check Yourself
  • Count every word in the gap before you finish.

Rule 4: Make the Whole Second Sentence Grammatical

We cancelled the picnic because of the rain.

DUE

The picnic was cancelled                  the rain.

✗ Wrong Answer

due it was

Problem: The sentence does not fit grammatically.

Correct Answer
due to
The picnic was cancelled due to the rain.

Extra Rule: Write Only the Missing Words

For the same question:

The picnic was cancelled __________ the rain.

Do not write: The picnic was cancelled due to the rain.

Write only: due to

Part 2 — Learn the Solving Method
Chapter 4

The Main Routine

Use this routine every time:

Meaning → Key Word → Gap Frame → Pattern → Answer → Check

Running Example

Someone stole my bike yesterday.

WAS

My bike                  yesterday.

Step 1 — Meaning

Ask:

  • Who or what is the sentence about?
  • What happened?
  • When did it happen?
  • Is the focus on the person doing the action, or the thing receiving the action?

Meaning: A person stole the bike. We do not know who, or the person is not important. The bike receives the action. The time is yesterday.

Step 2 — Key Word

Key word: WAS

This suggests a past passive form.

Step 3 — Gap Frame

Look at the second sentence like this:

My bike + gap + yesterday

The answer must fit after My bike and before yesterday.

Step 4 — Pattern

Passive pattern:

object + be + past participle

Here: My bike + was + stolen

Step 5 — Answer
Answer
was stolen
Step 6 — Check
My bike was stolen yesterday.
Check Yourself
  • Same meaning? Yes.
  • Key word unchanged? Yes: was.
  • Two to five words? Yes: two words.
  • Grammar fits? Yes.
  • Spelling correct? Yes: stolen.
Student Thinking Box

I do not know who stole it.
The bike receives the action.
So I need the passive.
The key word was fits the passive.
The past participle of steal is stolen.

Chapter 5

Understanding the Marking

Part 4 is not always just right or wrong. Each answer can receive up to two marks. Cambridge's handbook says the mark scheme splits the answer into two parts and candidates get one mark for each correct part.1

Official answer keys often show these two parts with a vertical line. For example, an answer key may show a Part 4 answer divided like this: have not / n't SEEN | Dan for.

Teaching Example

She has never visited Rome before.

BEEN

She                  Rome before.

Correct Answer
has never been to
She has never been to Rome before.

Possible marking view:

Part 1Part 2
has neverbeen to

What This Means for You

If you know part of the answer, do not leave the gap empty. A careful partial answer may be better than no answer. But do not write random words. Use what you know.

Check Yourself
  • Do I know the tense?
  • Do I know the fixed phrase?
  • Do I know the preposition?
  • Can I write at least the part I am sure about?

Passive Forms

A passive sentence starts with the person or thing that receives the action.

Active: Someone stole my bike.
Passive: My bike was stolen.

Common Key Words

was   were   is   are   been   be   by

Basic Pattern

object + be + past participle

Examples: was stolen / are cleaned / has been repaired / must be checked

Slow Demonstration

Someone stole my bike yesterday.

WAS

My bike                  yesterday.

Meaning

The bike receives the action.

Key Word

WAS suggests past passive.

Gap Frame

My bike + gap + yesterday

Pattern
was + past participle
Answer
Answer
was stolen
My bike was stolen yesterday.

Try One

They clean the classrooms every day.

ARE

The classrooms                  every day.

Answer
are cleaned
The classrooms are cleaned every day.

Modal Passive

modal + be + past participle

Examples: must be done / should be checked / can be seen

You must finish this report today.

BE

This report                  today.

Answer
must be finished
This report must be finished today.

Spot the Danger

✗ Wrong

My bike was stole yesterday.

Why wrong? After was, you need the past participle: stolen

✓ Correct
was stolen
My bike was stolen yesterday.

Mini Checklist

Check Yourself
  • Who or what receives the action?
  • Do I need am/is/are/was/were/be/been?
  • What is the past participle?
  • Is the time still the same?
  • Do I need by + person, or is it unnecessary?

Comparisons

Comparison questions ask you to keep the same comparison, even when the sentence changes shape. The subject may change. When the subject changes, the comparison may need to reverse.

Common Key Words

Basic Patterns

MeaningPattern

Slow Demonstration

NOT

The last book was                  this one.

Meaning

Key Word

NOT suggests a negative comparison.

Gap Frame

The adjective easy stays, but the subject changes. So the comparison reverses.

Try One

✗ Wrong

That means the two books were equally easy. The first sentence says this book was easier.

Check Yourself

Result means: What happened because of something?
Purpose means: Why did someone do something?

Basic Patterns

Meaning

Part 4 — Avoid Common Mistakes
Chapter 18

Danger Zones

These are common ways to lose marks.

1

Changing the Key Word

Key word: SUCCESS

Wrong: successful / succeeded / successfully

Rule
The sentence may change around the key word, but the key word itself must not change.
2

Writing Too Many Words

Part 4 answers must be two to five words. Cambridge also notes that contracted forms count as the full form for word-count purposes, with can't = cannot treated as one word.1

Exam-safe practice habit: Write did not, not didn't, unless your teacher or answer key clearly accepts the contraction.

I last saw Ana in 2020. SINCE

I              2020.

✓ Correct (five words)
have not seen Ana since
✗ Too long

have not had a meeting with Ana since

3

Changing the Meaning

Small changes can create a different idea.

Meaning AMeaning B
not necessaryprohibited
finished timeunfinished time
causeresult
activepassive
possiblecertain
adviceobligation

Example: You don't have to go. = It is not necessary.  |  You must not go. = It is not allowed.

4

Ignoring Words Before and After the Gap

Do not look only at the gap.

Example: She is looking forward __________ her cousins.

The words before and after show the pattern: looking forward to seeing her cousins

✗ Wrong

looking forward to see

✓ Correct
to seeing
5

Repeating Words Already Outside the Gap

The teacher gave us extra homework. WERE

We              extra homework.

✓ Correct
were given
We were given extra homework.
✗ Wrong

were given extra homework

Full sentence becomes: We were given extra homework extra homework. — You repeated words already outside the gap.

6

WrongCorrect

7

Singular/Plural Mistakes

Look at the whole second sentence. Sometimes a word after the gap shows whether the answer should be singular or plural.

Example: The student __________ the project. → has responsibility for

Example: The students __________ the project. → have responsibility for

Exam-Day Method

In the exam, you need a calm procedure. Cambridge teaching tips advise students to manage time carefully, not spend too long on one part, and leave a few minutes at the end to check answers.2

Exam Method

  1. Do the easier transformations first.
  2. Do not spend too long on one question.
  3. Write a partial answer if you know part of it.
  4. Check the key word.
  5. Count the words.
  6. Read the full second sentence silently.
  7. Check spelling.
  8. Return to skipped questions if time remains.

Decision Tree

Do I know the pattern?
→ Write it and check.


Do I partly know the pattern?
→ Write the part I know carefully.


Do I not know it?
→ Skip it and return later.

Final Check for Each Answer

Check Yourself
  • Same main meaning?
  • Key word unchanged?
  • Two to five words?
  • Grammar fits before and after the gap?
  • Spelling correct?
  • No extra repeated words?

Full Demonstration Walkthroughs

Use these examples to see the full thinking process.

1
Present Perfect / Since

I last visited Spain in 2019.

SINCE

I                  2019.

Step 1 — Meaning

The last visit was in 2019. From 2019 until now, I have not visited Spain.

Step 2 — Key Word

SINCE shows the starting point.

Step 3 — Gap Frame

I + gap + 2019

Step 4 — Pattern
have not + past participle + since
Step 5 — Answer
Answer
have not visited Spain since
I have not visited Spain since 2019.
Step 6 — Check
Check Yourself
  • Same meaning? Yes.
  • Key word unchanged? Yes.
  • Two to five words? Five.
  • Grammar correct? Yes.
  • Spelling correct? Yes.
Why Wrong Answers Are Wrong

did not visit Spain since — Use present perfect with since when the period continues until now.

have not visited since Spain — The word order is wrong.

2
Passive

They repaired my laptop yesterday.

WAS

My laptop                  yesterday.

Meaning

Someone repaired the laptop. The laptop receives the action.

Pattern
was + past participle
Answer
Answer
was repaired
My laptop was repaired yesterday.
Why Wrong Answers Are Wrong

was repair — After was, use the past participle: repaired.

were repaired — Laptop is singular, so use was.

3
Modal Obligation

It wasn't necessary for us to buy tickets.

HAVE

We                  tickets.

Meaning

Buying tickets was not necessary.

Pattern
did not have to + verb
Answer
Answer
did not have to buy
We did not have to buy tickets.
Why Wrong Answers Are Wrong

must not buy — This means it was prohibited. The first sentence means it was not necessary.

had not to buy — This is not the correct structure.

4
Comparison

Jenny is taller than Mark.

AS

Mark is                  Jenny.

Meaning

Jenny is taller. Mark is shorter.

Pattern
not as + adjective + as
Answer
Answer
not as tall as
Mark is not as tall as Jenny.
Why Wrong Answers Are Wrong

as tall as — This means they are the same height.

not taller as — The structure is wrong. Use not as tall as.

5
Gerund or Infinitive

I am excited about meeting your family.

FORWARD

I am                  your family.

Pattern
looking forward to meeting
Answer
Answer
looking forward to meeting
I am looking forward to meeting your family.
Why Wrong Answers Are Wrong

looking forward to meet — After look forward to, use -ing.

look forward meeting — The sentence needs am looking, and the expression needs to.

6
Contrast Linker

Despite the noise, I slept well.

ALTHOUGH

I slept well                 .

Pattern
although + subject + verb
Answer
Answer
although it was noisy
I slept well although it was noisy.
Why Wrong Answers Are Wrong

although the noise — After although, use a subject and verb.

despite it was noisy — After despite, do not use a full clause.

7
Result

The test was so hard that I couldn't finish it.

TOO

The test                 .

Pattern
too + adjective + to + verb
Answer
Answer
was too hard to finish
The test was too hard to finish.
Why Wrong Answers Are Wrong

was too hard that — Use so ... that, but too ... to.

was enough hard to finish — The word order is wrong, and the meaning changes.

8
Conditional

If you don't book now, there won't be any seats.

UNLESS

There won't be any seats                 .

Pattern
unless + positive subject + verb
Answer
Answer
unless you book now
There won't be any seats unless you book now.
Why Wrong Answers Are Wrong

unless you don't book now — Double negative meaning.

if you book now — This gives the opposite condition.

9
Reported Speech

"Don't be late," my teacher said to me.

WARNED

My teacher                  late.

Pattern
warned + person + not to + verb
Answer
Answer
warned me not to be
My teacher warned me not to be late.
Why Wrong Answers Are Wrong

warned me to not be — The safer exam pattern is warned me not to be.

warned I not to be — After warned, use object form: me.

10
Phrasal Verb

Jack arrived late for the meeting.

TURNED

Jack                  for the meeting.

Key Word

TURNED suggests the phrasal verb: turn up = arrive

Answer
Answer
turned up late
Jack turned up late for the meeting.
Why Wrong Answers Are Wrong

turned late up — The word order is unnatural here.

turned down late — Turn down has a different meaning.

11
B2 Verb Pattern

Maria said Liam had broken the window.

ACCUSED

Maria                  the window.

Pattern
accused + person + of + -ing
Answer
Answer
accused Liam of breaking
Maria accused Liam of breaking the window.
Why Wrong Answers Are Wrong

accused Liam to break — Use of + -ing, not to + infinitive.

accused that Liam broke — This is not the correct pattern for this sentence.

12
Mixed Pattern

Because I was ill, I missed the class.

PREVENTED

My illness                  the class.

Meaning

My illness stopped me from attending the class.

Pattern
prevented + person + from + -ing
Answer
Answer
prevented me from attending
My illness prevented me from attending the class.
Why Wrong Answers Are Wrong

prevented me to attend — After prevent someone, use from + -ing.

prevented from attending — The sentence needs the object me.
